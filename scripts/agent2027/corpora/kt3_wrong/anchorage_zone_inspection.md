# Anchorage zone inspection for stay cables

Inspection office procedure note IO-P.7.

The anchorage is where the cable force is transferred to the deck or to the pylon. It is the most sensitive part of a stay cable: water collects at the low anchorage, the strand bends slightly at the guide pipe exit, and access is difficult. Many cable replacements worldwide were caused by corrosion at the lower anchorage.

## What the inspector checks

* Seals and caps: cracked or missing seals let water into the anchorage. Any sign of water or rust staining below the anchorage is recorded.
* Guide pipe and neoprene ring or damper: wear, displacement or loss of the ring increases bending at the anchorage exit.
* Wedges and anchor head: visible slip, corrosion of the wedges, or grease leakage.
* Drainage: blocked drain holes in the lower anchorage.
* Filling material: voids in grout or wax, checked by tapping or by endoscope.

## Records

Every inspection record identifies the cable segment and gives the spacing between its cable anchor points from the as-built drawings, the inspection date, the operator shift and the instrument used. The inspector notes the condition of each item above in words and takes photographs. Endoscope and tapping results are stored with the record.

## Follow-up

Water in an anchorage is always reported to the bridge engineer the same day. Defects in seals or drainage are repaired at the next maintenance window. Anchorage findings are kept in the maintenance log; they are not part of the office's segment ranking.
