# Measuring the tension in stay-cable strands

Inspection office background note IO-B.2.

The tension in a stay cable changes with temperature, traffic, creep of the deck and any loss of strands. The inspection office measures it during each campaign and records the measured tension in the cable strand in kN.

## Methods

* Vibration (frequency) method: an accelerometer on the cable records its natural frequencies. For a taut string the tension follows from the fundamental frequency, the mass per unit length and the free vibrating length; corrections for bending stiffness, sag and the dampers are needed for accurate values. It is fast and needs no access to the anchorage.
* Lift-off test: a hydraulic jack at the anchorage lifts the anchor head or a single strand; the jack pressure at lift-off gives the force. It is direct but slow and needs access to the anchorage.
* Load cells: permanent load cells under the anchor head of selected strands give continuous readings.
* Magneto-elastic sensors: a coil around the strand measures the change of magnetic permeability with stress.

## Accuracy and corrections

Readings are corrected to a reference temperature, because the cable and the deck expand differently with temperature. Tension readings with the vibration method are less reliable for short cables and for cables with heavy dampers. A single strand reading that differs strongly from its neighbours may indicate slipped wedges or a broken wire.

## Use of the readings

The design tension of each cable is known from the construction records. A drop in tension against the design value is reported to the bridge engineer, as it may mean strand loss or anchorage slip. Tension readings are stored in the inspection record of each segment.
