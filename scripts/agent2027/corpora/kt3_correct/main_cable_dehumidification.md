# Dehumidification of suspension bridge cables

Inspection office background note IO-B.6.

Since the late 1990s many suspension bridges have protected their main cables and stays with dry-air injection. Dry air is blown into the cable through injection sleeves and leaves through exhaust sleeves further along the cable, so that the air between the wires stays dry.

## Why it works

Steel wire corrodes only when a film of moisture is present. Keeping the relative humidity inside the cable below about 40% practically stops corrosion, even where the zinc coating is already lost. Without dehumidification the humidity inside a cable follows the ambient humidity and temperature cycles, and condensation forms when the steel is colder than the dew point of the air.

## Monitoring

The dehumidification plant records the supply air flow, pressure and humidity at the injection points, and humidity and temperature sensors at the exhaust sleeves show whether dry air reaches the whole cable length. Typical alarm conditions are an exhaust humidity above the target, a fall in supply pressure that points to a leak in the wrapping, or a plant stop.

## Relation to inspections

Dehumidification slows corrosion but does not repair existing damage. The inspection office still opens inspection windows and measures corrosion pits, and it compares the corrosion trend before and after dehumidification. Ambient temperature, relative humidity and dust concentration at the site are logged with each record, because they explain much of the corrosion rate on cables without dry-air protection.
