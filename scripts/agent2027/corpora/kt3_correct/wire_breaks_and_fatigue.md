# Wire breaks and fatigue in stay cables

Inspection office background note IO-B.4.

A stay cable is made of many parallel wires or strands, so the loss of a single wire does not endanger the bridge. Broken wires are nevertheless the clearest sign that a cable is deteriorating, and their number and location are tracked over the service life.

## Causes of wire breaks

* Fatigue: traffic and wind cause repeated stress cycles. Bending at the anchorage exit and at clamps adds local stresses. Fatigue cracks usually start at a surface defect.
* Corrosion fatigue: a corrosion pit acts as a notch and shortens the fatigue life considerably.
* Hydrogen embrittlement: high-strength wires in a corrosive environment can crack under sustained stress.
* Mechanical damage: impact during construction or maintenance.

## Detection

* Acoustic monitoring: sensors along the cable record the sound of a wire break; the time difference between sensors locates it.
* Magnetic flux leakage: a magnet head travels along the cable and detects loss of metallic cross-section and broken wires inside the cable.
* Visual inspection at opened windows.

## Use of the findings

The number of broken wires per cable and per segment is reported to the bridge engineer, who compares it with the allowable loss of cross-section. A cluster of breaks in one segment, or a rising break rate, triggers a special inspection. Wire break counts are stored in the maintenance log together with the inspection count of the segment.
