# Stay-cable vibration and damping: what the vibration readings mean

Inspection office background note IO-B.3.

Stay cables are long, light and lightly damped, so they vibrate easily. The vibration sensors on the bridge report an RMS vibration velocity in mm/s for each instrumented location. This note explains the main vibration mechanisms and how the readings are used.

## Mechanisms

* Rain-wind induced vibration: rivulets of rain water on the cable surface change its aerodynamic shape and can excite large-amplitude oscillations at moderate wind speeds. It is the most common cause of complaints from bridge users.
* Vortex shedding: periodic vortices behind the cable excite it when the shedding frequency matches a natural frequency of the cable. Amplitudes are usually small.
* Parametric excitation: movement of the deck or the pylon at about twice a cable frequency can pump energy into the cable.
* Galloping of iced or dry cables: rare, but possible for unusual cross-sections or ice accretion.

## Damping

The sensitivity of a cable to these mechanisms is usually discussed with the Scruton number, a mass-damping parameter that grows with the mass per unit length and with the damping ratio of the cable and falls with air density and cable diameter. A higher Scruton number means a cable is less prone to rain-wind and vortex-induced vibration. External dampers near the anchorages, helical surface fillets and cross-ties between cables are the common counter-measures.

## Use of the readings

The RMS vibration velocity is trended per location. A sudden rise in vibration after a damper inspection or replacement points to a damper fault. Vibration readings are logged together with ambient temperature and humidity because wet cables behave differently from dry ones. Vibration levels are recorded for monitoring purposes; they are not part of the office's segment ranking.
