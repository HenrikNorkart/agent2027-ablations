### Final focus handed to the CODER

**Focus (exploitative):** Introduce a set of higher‑order and rate‑based physics features that are orthogonal to the existing stress‑proxy family, plus latent‑factor components from the raw numeric columns.

1. **Non‑linear stress terms** – `stress_proxy_sq = stress_proxy ** 2`, `log_stress_proxy_sq`, and `stress_proxy_cubic = stress_proxy ** 3`.  
2. **Load‑rate features** – `load_rate = axial_load_kn / operating_hours_total` (and its `log`).  
3. **Vibration‑rate features** – `vib_rate = vibration_rms_mm_s / operating_hours_total` (and its `log`).  
4. **Cumulative‑vibration load** – `cum_vib_load = vibration_rms_mm_s * operating_hours_total` (and its `log`).  
5. **Interaction of non‑linear stress with environment** – `stress_proxy_sq * relative_humidity_pct` and `log(stress_proxy_sq * ambient_temperature_c)`.  
6. **Latent‑factor PCA components** – compute a PCA on the continuous numeric attributes (excluding `stress_proxy` and its transforms) and append the first two principal component scores (`pca_comp_1`, `pca_comp_2`).

*Justification:* The latest assessment shows that predictive power is concentrated in the stress‑proxy family while many transformed and categorical columns add noise and severe multicollinearity. Adding polynomial and rate‑based versions of the core stress variable provides orthogonal, physics‑motivated signals that can capture non‑linear fatigue and loading effects without inflating VIF. PCA‑derived components supply a low‑dimensional summary of the remaining correlated continuous variables, reducing redundancy. These purely additive features give the Coder concrete new attributes for the Evaluator to evaluate, aiming to improve RMSE while respecting the current constraints.
