### Final focus handed to the CODER

**Focus (exploitative):** Introduce physics‑inspired fatigue‑damage proxy features that combine the member’s stress state, vibration energy, and usage time.

1. **Compute base proxies**  
   - `axial_stress = axial_load_kn / (cross_section_cm2 * 1e‑4)` (kN/m²) – already useful, add its log.  
   - `vibration_energy = vibration_rms_mm_s ** 2` and `log_vibration_energy = np.log1p(vibration_energy)`.

2. **Create cumulative‑damage proxies**  
   - `damage_proxy = operating_hours_total * axial_stress * vibration_energy`  
   - `log_damage_proxy = np.log1p(damage_proxy)`.

3. **Add usage‑maintenance interaction**  
   - `hours_per_maintenance = operating_hours_total / maintenance_interval_days` (captures exposure between maintenances).  
   - `damage_proxy_per_maint = damage_proxy / maintenance_interval_days`.

4. **Add environment‑stress interactions** (orthogonal to existing ones)  
   - `stress_temp = axial_stress * ambient_temperature_c`  
   - `stress_humidity = axial_stress * relative_humidity_pct`.

**Justification:** The Evaluator’s assessment shows that the strongest predictors are vibration‑load interactions, but they are highly collinear (VIF ≫ 10⁴). By aggregating vibration energy, axial stress, and cumulative operating time into a single “damage proxy” we introduce an orthogonal, physics‑based signal that captures fatigue‑type degradation over the specimen’s service life. The additional ratios with maintenance interval expose the effect of inspection/maintenance scheduling, while the simple stress‑environment products add low‑VIF, potentially informative variability. All requested attributes are additive and can be generated with pandas/numpy, satisfying the Coder’s capabilities.
